Propensity Score Balance Explorer

See how matching, weighting, and stratification use propensity scores to balance covariates between treated and control groups, and when they cannot.

1. Dataset

Propensity scores are illustrative model scores from a fixed formula, e(x) = 1 / (1 + exp(−(−4 + 0.06·Age + 0.06·BMI))). They are not estimated from this sample, and treatment labels are hard-coded rather than drawn from these probabilities. No outcome is modeled, so this page demonstrates covariate imbalance and its adjustment, not bias in an effect estimate.

Balance Check: Standardized Mean Difference (SMD)

Goal: |SMD| < 0.1 (inside the dashed band). For every method, SMD = (adjusted treated mean − adjusted control mean) divided by the unadjusted pooled SD, √((sT² + sC²)/2), so values are comparable across methods. The axis uses one fixed scale for all methods on the selected dataset.

Weighting: Bars show weighted means. Treated individuals receive weight 1/e(x) and controls 1/(1 − e(x)), creating a pseudo-population that targets the average treatment effect (ATE). Effective sample sizes are shown at left.
Matching: Greedy 1:1 nearest-neighbor matching without replacement, restricted to pairs within the caliper. Treated individuals without an acceptable control are excluded, so the matched sample describes only the matched treated individuals.
Stratification: Individuals are split into 4 equal-count strata by propensity score. Adjusted means average the within-stratum means using the same stratum weights (share of individuals in usable strata) for both groups. Strata lacking either group are excluded and listed below.
